Traditional Food, Major Resources & Herbs of Malaysia

Malaysia’s daily life is shaped by land, sea, forests and farms as well as food. This page explains how selected resources are produced, how they are used and how to think about herbs safely.

Herbs are not a replacement for medical treatment. Traditional use does not automatically mean a product is proven to treat disease. If pregnant, breastfeeding, taking medication or managing a health condition, ask a doctor or pharmacist before using concentrated extracts or supplements.

Traditional food: what shapes Malaysian flavour?

Rice & coconut milk

Nasi lemak and rice dishes

Rice is a staple; coconut milk, sambal, fish, ulam and local side dishes create many variations across states and communities.

Spices & gravies

Curry, rendang and masak lemak

Spices, onion, ginger, lemongrass, turmeric and coconut milk reflect interaction between Malay, Indian, Chinese and wider Nusantara traditions.

Sea & river

Fish, budu, cincalok and keropok

Preservation and processing of seafood help food last longer and create distinctive local flavours.

Multicultural heritage

Nyonya, mamak, kopitiam and Borneo traditions

Malaysian food grew through cultural exchange; recipes may be shared and adapted across families and regions.

Palm oil: from fruit to everyday products

Oil palm is an important Malaysian industrial crop. Its oil is used in food, soap, cosmetics and other products. Quality and sustainability depend on how farms and mills are managed.

Crude palm oil

Produced from the flesh of the oil-palm fruit before further refining or processing.

Palm kernel oil

Think about the whole diet

When judging a food product, consider overall nutrition, cooking method and portion size rather than labelling one oil as simply “good” or “bad”.

Other major Malaysian resources

Agriculture

Paddy and rice

Paddy cultivation supplies rice and involves seeding, growing, harvesting, drying and milling.

Plantations

Rubber

Latex is tapped from rubber trees, collected and processed for many industrial uses.

Food

Cocoa, coconut & tropical fruit

Cocoa becomes chocolate; coconuts provide milk and oil; durian, pineapple and other tropical fruits support agriculture and food tourism.

Borneo

Sarawak pepper

Pepper is harvested, processed and dried for flavour and aroma in food products.

Herbs and plants: use them with knowledge

Cooking

Turmeric, ginger, lemongrass & pegaga

Commonly used in food, drinks and traditional practices; as ordinary foods they add flavour and variety.

Traditional use

Tongkat ali & kacip fatimah

Known in Malay traditional use and often sold as supplements. Safety, dosage, purity and drug interactions matter, especially for concentrated extracts.

Herbal drinks

Misai kucing & mengkudu

Used in traditional practice and herbal drinks. Do not assume they treat disease without professional health advice.

Biodiversity

Identify species correctly

Local plant names can vary. Do not harvest or consume wild plants without accurate identification.

Choosing herbal products more safely

  1. Avoid products promising a cure or extraordinary results very quickly.
  2. Check the label, ingredients, warnings, relevant registration details and a contactable manufacturer.
  3. Avoid stacking many supplements, especially if you take medication.
  4. Stop use and seek medical advice if you experience an unusual reaction.
